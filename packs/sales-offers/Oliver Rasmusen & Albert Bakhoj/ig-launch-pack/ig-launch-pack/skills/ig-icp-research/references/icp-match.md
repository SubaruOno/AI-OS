# The ICP-Match Check

The single most important filter in this pack. Without it the research looks perfect and sends the member into a wall.

---

## The problem it solves

A member selling AI intake software to law firms ran the research. It returned exactly what a good result looks like:

| Account | Followers | Views | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| @mehdipourlaw | 2.5K | 174K | 42.5x |
| @kiranrajkumarr | 1.2K | 128.2K | 30.1x |
| @lobo_law_pllc | 2.7K | 119.4K | 19.8x |
| @valleyinjuryteam | 7.8K | 101K | 14.8x |

Small accounts. Big multipliers. Every signal says green.

Then read the `audience.demographics` field on those results:

- "Adults interested in legal services"
- "Potential clients"

**Those lawyers are making content to attract injury victims.** They are not talking to other firm owners. Copying their formats builds an audience of people who need a lawyer, not people who buy software.

The two results that did target lawyers reached law students and first-year associates. Law students do not buy intake systems.

That member would have filmed daily for 30 days and closed nothing, and would not have noticed until month two.

---

## The check

Every result returned by an outlier search carries an `audience.demographics` field. Classify all of them into three buckets before anything else happens.

### Bucket 1: THE ICP. Build from these.

The audience is the person who writes the cheque.

- "Gym owners, aspiring entrepreneurs"
- "HVAC professionals and business owners"
- "Dental practice owners and clinical staff"
- "Business owners in the trades or home services"
- "Practice owners and administrative staff"

### Bucket 2: THE ICP'S CUSTOMERS. Drop these.

The audience is who your buyer sells to. This content reaches consumers.

- "Potential clients"
- "Adults interested in legal services"
- "Adults interested in dental aesthetics"
- "Homeowners, DIYers"
- "Fitness enthusiasts, 18-35"

These often have the biggest numbers in the whole sweep. Ignore the numbers. Wrong room.

### Bucket 3: ADJACENT. Flag, do not build from.

Includes **aspirants**, which is the trap almost everyone falls into.

Aspirants are people who want to be your buyer but are not yet. "Aspiring real estate agents." "People starting an HVAC business." "Law students." They are technically in the industry, which makes them look like the ICP, and they buy nothing because they have no business yet.

Content aimed at aspirants gets big numbers because there are far more people dreaming about a business than running one. Every niche has this pool and it is always bigger than the real one.

Test: does this audience already have the problem your offer solves? An aspiring agent has no leads to follow up on. Flag it.

The audience works in the industry but cannot buy.

- "Law students, early-career attorneys"
- "Dental students"
- "Technicians and tradespeople" when selling to owners
- "Front desk and admin staff" when selling to the practice owner

Sometimes useful for warmup targeting, because staff and owners overlap in comment sections. Never the basis of a content calendar.

## The anomaly guard

A multiplier above roughly 500x is a lottery ticket, not a template.

Real example. A real estate agent with 5,100 followers posted a reel about bad leads and it did **22.7 million views at 14,657x**. Nothing about that reel explains the result. It caught an algorithmic wave that will not repeat.

If the research hands a member that reel as their number one format to copy, they will copy it, do 900 views, and conclude the system does not work.

**Rule.** Anything above 500x gets flagged as an anomaly. Use it as evidence that the pain is real, because a reel like that proves the topic hits. Never present it as a repeatable format.

The reels worth copying sit between 10x and 200x. That range is where a format is genuinely working rather than getting lucky.

## Reporting

State the split out loud, always:

```
ICP-match: 6 of 18 results

  ICP                    6   built from
  ICP's customers        9   dropped
  Adjacent               3   flagged
```

If the member cannot see this, they cannot trust the output.

---

## The verdict

Day 1 ends with a decision, not a document.

### GREEN
Enough ICP-bucket results to fill a calendar. Pain content exists. Offer proof exists. Barriers clearable.
→ Build the 10 reel launch plan.

### AMBER
ICP pain content exists but no proven reel selling this kind of offer to this ICP.
→ Proceed, but they are first to this angle. Weight toward advice content, fewer build reels, expect a slower ramp. Say this plainly.

### RED
Either the pool is dominated by the ICP's own customers, or the ICP is not on Instagram in a business context at all.
→ **Tell them not to do it.** Then give them the two real options:
1. Same offer, different ICP. Suggest the specific niches where the pain sweep did come back clean.
2. Keep the ICP, drop Instagram. Put the effort into cold email or cold calling, and use the pain list from this research for the subject lines and openers. The research is still worth having.

**Never soften a RED into an AMBER.** A member who is told "not this one, and here is why" on day one has been saved 30 days of daily filming. That is the most valuable output this skill can produce.

## Known RED and GREEN patterns

**Reliably GREEN.** Home services (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing), gyms and fitness studios, restaurants and food service, auto and detailing, contractors and trades, med spas and salons.

These owners post about their own business publicly and follow accounts that advise them.

**Usually RED or AMBER.** Law firms, medical and dental practices, accounting firms, insurance agencies.

Two reasons. Their industry content on Instagram is aimed at their own patients and clients. And the owners themselves are on LinkedIn, not Instagram, in a business context.

This is a pattern, not a law. Run the sweep anyway. The data decides, not the list.
