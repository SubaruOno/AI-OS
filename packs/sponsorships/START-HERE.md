## What this folder is, and what it isn't

Akil Wade handed over two skills that work as a pair. One scores whether a business is sponsor-ready and tells it what to fix first. The other takes something that already has an audience and turns it into a sponsor-ready offer with a target list, custom activation angles and outreach.

They came out of running real events and real sponsor conversations. The frameworks carry that: the scoring weights, the activation menu, the outreach templates all reflect one operator's experience of what brands actually said yes to.

**Treat this folder as a signal bank.** The scoring rubric isn't objective truth, it's a well-argued opinion about what makes a brand worth sponsoring. The value is in the shape of the argument, not the specific point totals.

Worth noting: the readiness skill carries its own privacy rule telling Claude not to reference Akil's brands or workspaces unless the user supplies them. He built these to travel, which makes them among the cleanest packs of the day to adapt.

---

## The rule

**Adapt, don't install.** Three honest outcomes:

1. **Take it as-is.** Both are self-contained markdown skills, so this is genuinely viable.
2. **Take the framework, reweight it.** Likely if their business is a different shape from an event. A newsletter, a podcast and a live event all get sponsored, but not for the same reasons.
3. **Take nothing, keep the note.** They have no audience and no event, so sponsorship is two years away. Say so and write down what would change that.

## Step 1: load the business first

Read quietly before opening anything:

- `CLAUDE.md`
- `context/business.md`, `context/you.md`, `context/offer.md`
- `context/strategy.md`, `context/numbers.md`, `context/team.md`
- `context/tech-stack.md`

Five things decide this session:

- **What's the sponsorable asset?** A YouTube channel, a newsletter, a podcast, a community, a live event, a course cohort. If there isn't one, say so early rather than scoring a thing that doesn't exist.
- **How big, honestly?** From `numbers.md` if it's there. Subscribers, listeners, attendees, open rate. Sponsors buy access to attention, and the real number matters more than the flattering one.
- **Who's the audience?** Founders, creators, operators, investors, developers. This is what a sponsor is actually buying, and it's usually worth more than raw size. A thousand founders beats fifty thousand teenagers for most B2B sponsors.
- **Have they ever taken sponsor money?** First deal and tenth deal are different conversations.
- **What proof exists?** Photos, videos, testimonials, past attendee lists, screenshots, press. Both skills lean on proof, and most people have more of it than they think.

If those files don't exist, ask five short questions and carry on.

## Step 2: survey what's actually in here

### `sponsor-ready-score-os/`

The diagnostic. It interviews, scores the business out of 1,000 across ten categories worth 100 each (positioning, audience, proof, content engine, offer clarity, sponsor value, brand trust, and three more), identifies why sponsors would or wouldn't trust them yet, and builds a plan to raise the score.

The opinion baked in: don't chase sponsors, score the asset first. Most people who can't land sponsors don't have a sales problem, they have a readiness problem, and the score makes that visible instead of arguable.

Ships with a scoring rubric, a readiness framework, a 30-day plan template, intake questions, a report template and a tracker CSV.

*Assumes:* something with an audience to score. It works for a creator, a founder, a community builder or an event operator, which is a wider net than the other skill.

### `sponsor-magnet-os/`

The offensive half. Identifies the right sponsors, infers what each one actually wants, builds a custom activation angle, creates proof visuals and drafts outreach.

Its core argument is the useful part: **sponsors don't only buy reach.** They buy qualified leads, product adoption, reusable content, market entry, community trust, brand association, customer education, founder relationships, recruiting signal, case studies, sales conversations, or high-status positioning. The instruction is explicit about not leading with generic tiers, and instead working out which outcome matters most to each sponsor and making the asset look like the obvious vehicle for it.

Ships with asset briefs, outreach templates, a prompt bank, a strategy reference, a roundtable script, an activation menu, sponsor call notes, a fit scorecard and a target tracker.

*Assumes:* an event, most heavily. The inputs it asks for are event name, date, location, audience size, ticket price and format. For a newsletter or channel, that framing needs translating.

## Step 3: name the gap out loud

Show a ranked view in their language, with their real numbers.

Something like:

> Run the readiness score first. From `numbers.md` you've got 4,200 subscribers with a 41% open rate and an audience that's mostly agency owners, which is a category brands pay well for. My guess before we start is you score high on audience and low on proof, because you've never packaged anything a sponsor could look at.
>
> The magnet skill assumes an event. You run a newsletter. The structure ports fine, but "audience size, ticket price, event format" becomes "subscriber count, open rate, placement format", and the activation menu needs rebuilding for what you can actually offer: a dedicated send, a sponsored section, a case study.
>
> His outreach templates are written in his voice. We'll rewrite them in yours, because a template that doesn't sound like you gets replies you can't live up to.

## Step 4: pick one and go deep

Run the readiness score first, almost always. It takes less time, it produces a number they'll remember, and it tells you whether the second skill is even worth running today.

If they score low, the session becomes the 30-day plan and that's the right outcome. If they score high, go on to the target list and one piece of outreach that's genuinely sendable.

---

## Step 5: adapt and build

- **Reweight the rubric for their asset.** Ten equal categories is a sensible default and a wrong one for some businesses. A podcast lives or dies on audience quality; a live event lives on proof. Change the weights with them and say why.
- **Translate the event vocabulary** if they don't run events. Same questions, different nouns.
- **Rewrite outreach in their voice.** Pull the tone from `you.md` and anything they've actually written. This is the single highest-value adaptation on this table.
- **Use their real proof.** Their screenshots, their testimonials, their numbers. A fit scorecard filled with hypotheticals is a waste of everyone's afternoon.
- **Be honest about the score.** The skill's value comes from telling people what's not working. If it comes back at 430, don't soften it. Deliver it straight and go to the plan.
- **Keep his privacy rule.** It's already in the skill. Don't strip it while editing.
- **Rewrite trigger phrases** to match how they talk.
- **Update `CLAUDE.md`** with whatever landed.

## If they're doing this at home

Same process. Load the context first. Have their real numbers to hand, since a score built on guessed metrics tells them nothing.

If the score comes back low, that's the session working. The plan is the deliverable, not the number.
