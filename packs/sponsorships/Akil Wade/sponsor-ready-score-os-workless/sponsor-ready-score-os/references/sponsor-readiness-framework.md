# Sponsor Readiness Framework

## What Sponsors Are Really Buying

Sponsors are rarely buying "support."

They are buying:

- access to a specific audience
- trust transfer
- content they can reuse
- proof that their product belongs in a category
- market entry
- leads or demos
- association with culture
- authority in front of buyers
- adoption moments

The user must become a vehicle for one or more of those outcomes.

## The Three Layers

### Layer 1: Identity

Can people explain what the user does in one sentence?

If not, sponsors will struggle to justify the partnership internally.

### Layer 2: Proof

Can the user show that they create attention, trust, community, or business outcomes?

If not, they need to create proof before asking for serious sponsor money.

### Layer 3: Sponsor Outcome

Can the user design a sponsor moment that gives the brand something useful?

If not, they only have a donation request, not a sponsorship offer.

## The Upgrade Path

Most people should not start by asking for a large sponsorship.

They should raise their score by building:

1. A clearer public positioning statement
2. A proof page or recap asset
3. A sponsor one-pager
4. A target sponsor list
5. One custom activation idea per sponsor category
6. A simple operations tracker
7. A follow-up and reporting system

## The Best First Sponsors

The best first sponsor is not always the biggest brand.

Look for a sponsor that:

- already wants the audience
- needs content
- has a current campaign
- can move quickly
- can benefit from a small activation
- has a human decision maker reachable by warm path
