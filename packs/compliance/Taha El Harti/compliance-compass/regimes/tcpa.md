## Does this apply to you? (triggers)

Any business that calls or texts US consumers for marketing — autodialed/prerecorded
calls and marketing SMS need **prior express written consent**. No size threshold.
Zero marketing texts/calls → NOT APPLICABLE; re-check the moment anyone proposes an
SMS campaign, because exposure starts with message one.

## Who can punish you, and how

**Invert the usual penalties framing: the adversary is a plaintiffs' firm at $500–
$1,500 per text, not the FCC.** Private right of action, uncapped, per message
(verified 2026-07-28) — among the highest-volume consumer class actions in the US.
A 10,000-message campaign without proper consent = $5M+ theoretical exposure.

2025 state of play (verified 2026-07-28): FCC's one-to-one consent rule **vacated**
(11th Cir., Jan 24, 2025; later formally repealed) — written consent still required,
just not the 1:1 seller-specific form. **Revocation rules effective Apr 11, 2025:**
consumers may revoke by any reasonable means (replying STOP, free text); honor within
**10 business days**. *McLaughlin v. McKesson* (SCOTUS, Jun 2025) freed district
courts from FCC interpretations — expect more litigation variance. State "mini"
telemarketing laws (FL, OK, WA) add stricter rules on top.

## Penalties & enforcement reality

**Honesty statement:** litigation-driven enforcement demonstrably reaches companies
of every size — per-message statutory damages make small senders economically worth
suing. Arguably the highest per-dollar-of-marketing risk regime a startup touches.

## Gap-check questions

1. Do you have a written consent record for every phone number you text for marketing?
2. Is STOP/opt-out handling automated, and honored within 10 business days by any
   reasonable revocation method?
3. Do you scrub against do-not-call lists and respect quiet hours?
4. Do you text into Florida, Oklahoma, or Washington (stricter state rules)?
5. Did you buy, rent, or inherit any phone list you can't trace consent for?

## First steps

1. Freeze any campaign lacking provable written consent (immediate).
2. Automate STOP handling end to end; test it (hours).
3. Keep consent records (who, when, what they agreed to) permanently (hours).
4. DNC scrubbing + quiet-hours rules in the sending platform (hours).
