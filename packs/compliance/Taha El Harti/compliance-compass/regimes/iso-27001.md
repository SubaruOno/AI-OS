## Does this apply to you? (triggers)

Like SOC 2, this is **buyer-driven**. It applies when procurement asks for it — and who
asks is largely a function of geography:

- **EU/UK/international enterprise buyers** commonly make ISO 27001 a hard procurement
  requirement; it also pairs naturally with a GDPR accountability posture.
- **US buyers ask for SOC 2 instead** — an ISO certificate alone often triggers "do you
  also have SOC 2?"

Does NOT apply (yet): no enterprise buyers, or US-only enterprise pipeline (SOC 2 is the
right instrument there — see `soc2.md`). NOT APPLICABLE, not "green"; re-check when
international enterprise deals enter the pipeline.

**The routing question for the interview: "Where are your next 10 enterprise deals — US
or international?"** US → SOC 2. International → ISO 27001. Both → see sequencing below.

## Who can punish you, and how

No regulator or fine. Punishment is failed procurement: international enterprise buyers
that treat the certificate as a gate. Once a signed contract requires you to maintain
certification, losing it becomes breach of contract.

**Bucket note:** market-expected until an MSA names it, then contract-mandated for that
customer. The report assigns the bucket per founder.

## Penalties & enforcement reality

- Statutory maxima: none — not law.
- What actually happens: deals blocked at procurement; certificates can be suspended or
  withdrawn by the certification body if surveillance audits are failed or skipped.
- Honesty statement: enforcement is entirely commercial; no small-company legal exposure
  exists for lacking it.

**What it is (vs. SOC 2):** unlike SOC 2, ISO 27001 **is a genuine certification**. An
accredited certification body audits your Information Security Management System — ISMS,
the documented system for how you manage security risk — in two stages (Stage 1
documentation review, Stage 2 implementation audit) and issues a certificate valid 3
years, with annual surveillance audits and a recertification audit at year 3. Certificates
are publicly claimable, and buyers can verify the issuer's accreditation. So: ISO gives
you a badge; SOC 2 gives you a confidential evidence report.

**Version status (verified 2026-07-28):** the 2013→2022 transition ended 31 October 2025.
All 2013 certificates are now expired/invalid; anyone who missed the deadline must do a
full initial certification against 2022. As of 2026 there is exactly one live version:
2022, with the reorganized 93-control Annex A (adds threat intelligence, cloud security,
secure coding, data leakage prevention). No transition guidance is needed or should be
given.

**Doing both SOC 2 and ISO 27001:** automation platforms map controls across both, making
the second framework incremental rather than additive. One credible school says ISO first
(the management-system layer makes SOC 2 nearly free afterward); the pragmatic startup
default remains "whichever the live deal demands" — usually SOC 2 first for US-centric
startups.

## Gap-check questions

1. Are your next enterprise deals mostly in the US, or in the EU/UK/elsewhere?
2. Has a prospect named ISO 27001 specifically in a contract, tender, or questionnaire?
3. Do you already hold a SOC 2 report? (Shared controls make ISO much cheaper to add.)
4. Do you have a written, current inventory of your security risks and who owns each one
   (the "risk assessment" at the heart of an ISMS)?
5. Has anyone internal ever formally audited your own security practices (an "internal
   audit" — a required part of the standard)?
6. Does leadership review security status on a schedule (a "management review" — also
   required)?
7. If you were certified before: was your certificate reissued against the 2022 version
   before 31 October 2025? (2013 certificates are now invalid.)

## First steps

1. Answer the routing question — if the pipeline is US-only, do SOC 2 instead and park
   this file. (Hours.)
2. If international demand is real: run a platform gap scan against ISO 27001:2022.
   (Days.)
3. Build the ISMS layer the scan will flag — risk assessment methodology, Statement of
   Applicability, internal audit, management review. This is the extra work vs. SOC 2.
   (Weeks.)
4. Select an accredited certification body early and book Stage 1/Stage 2. (Days to
   select; lead times matter.)
5. Calendar the annual surveillance audits — missing them is how certificates lapse.
   (Hours.)

## Cost baselines (2026, verified 2026-07-28)

Sources are auditors, platforms, and consultancies with pricing incentives — auditors skew
high, platforms skew low. Planning bands, not quotes.

- Certification audit fees (Stage 1+2), startup scale: ~$12k–$25k.
- Total first certification, small SaaS: $15k–$50k typical.
- 3-year total cost of ownership, 10–150-person SaaS (surveillance audits + platform +
  internal time): ~$35k–$120k.
- Internal effort: ~200–500 hours first time (vs. ~150–400 for a first SOC 2 Type 2) —
  the ISMS management-system layer is the delta.
- Timeline: 6–15 months.
- SOC 2 + ISO 27001 together: ~$30k–$150k audit fees over 12–24 months; bundling both
  with one firm saves ~20–35%.
