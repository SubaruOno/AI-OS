# Writing Style Reference

> Mandatory rules for all workspace output. Loaded every session through prime.
> Research basis: `reference/research/anti-ai-slop-master.md` (70+ sources, 15 YouTube transcripts, 5 GitHub repos)

---

## The 12 Core Rules

1. **Ban AI vocabulary.** Never use Tier 1 words (see list below). They appear at 50-150x the rate of human writers.
2. **No em dashes.** Zero. Use commas, periods, or parentheses. Em dashes are the single biggest AI tell, baked into training data from 19th-century books.
3. **Use contractions.** "Don't" not "do not." "It's" not "it is." "We've" not "we have." Write how people actually talk.
4. **Vary sentence length.** Mix 5-word and 30-word sentences. Never stack three or more similar-length sentences consecutively. AI clusters at 15-25 words; humans swing 3-40+.
5. **Start with content, not setup.** First sentence must contain real information. No throat-clearing.
6. **No binary contrast formulas.** Never write "It's not X, it's Y" or "The answer isn't X. It's Y." State the point directly.
7. **No dramatic fragmentation.** "Short. Punchy. Exhausting." is an AI pattern. Write complete sentences.
8. **Break the rule of three.** Don't default to exactly three items. Use two, four, or irregular groupings.
9. **Be specific.** Replace vague adjectives with concrete details, numbers, names, timeframes.
10. **No filler transitions.** Cut "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "It's worth noting that," "In conclusion."
11. **No chatbot artifacts.** Never write "I hope this helps!", "Great question!", "Certainly!", "Would you like me to," or assistant-speak.
12. **Vary paragraph length.** Some one sentence. Some five or six. Never uniform blocks.

## Banned Words

### Tier 1: Never Use

**Verbs:** delve, embark, endeavour, leverage, harness, navigate (metaphorical), unlock, unleash, foster, underscore, showcase, illuminate, elucidate, tackle, streamline, optimize, empower, reimagine, revolutionize, spearhead, capitalize, forge

**Adjectives:** robust, seamless, groundbreaking, transformative, cutting-edge, pivotal, paramount, multifaceted, comprehensive, tailored, scalable, dynamic, agile, next-generation, best-in-class, unprecedented, vibrant, intricate, compelling, invaluable, remarkable, nuanced, innovative, elevated

**Nouns:** landscape (metaphorical), tapestry, realm, testament, journey (metaphorical), intersection, synergy, paradigm, ecosystem (non-technical), toolkit (metaphorical)

**Adverbs:** deeply, truly, fundamentally, inherently, simply, inevitably, crucially, importantly, interestingly, significantly, notably

### Tier 2: Avoid Clustering

Fine once in a long document. Suspicious when two or more appear near each other.

**Transitions:** Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Indeed, Subsequently, Accordingly, Consequently, Thus

**Phrases:** "It's worth noting that", "It is important to note that", "In order to" (just say "to"), "At the end of the day", "When it comes to", "At its core", "The reality is", "Due to the fact that" (say "because")

## Banned Phrases

### Openers (never start a section or document with these)
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In today's digital landscape..."
- "As technology continues to evolve..."
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "As we navigate the complexities of..."
- "In a world where..."

### Closers (never end a section or document with these)
- "In conclusion..."
- "In summary..."
- "In essence..."
- "The future remains bright"
- "Only time will tell"
- "One thing is certain"

### Promotional fluff
- "Unlock your full potential"
- "Revolutionize the way"
- "Take your X to the next level"
- "Push the boundaries"
- "Harness the power of"
- "Embark on a journey"
- "At the forefront of"
- "Game-changer" / "Future-proof" / "Supercharge"

### Setup and emphasis crutches
- "Here's the thing:"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "Full stop." / "Period." / "Let that sink in."

### Chatbot artifacts (zero tolerance)
- "I hope this helps!"
- "Of course!" / "Certainly!" / "Absolutely!"
- "You're absolutely right!"
- "Great question!"
- "Would you like me to..." / "Is there anything else..."
- "Let me know if..." / "I'd be happy to..."
- "Feel free to reach out"

## Structural Patterns to Avoid

### Binary Contrasts
The single most overused AI formula. Never write:
- "It's not X. It's Y."
- "The answer isn't X. It's Y."
- "Not because X. Because Y."
- "stops being X and starts being Y"

Just state the point.

### Dramatic Fragmentation
- "[Noun]. That's it. That's the [thing]."
- "X. And Y. And Z." (staccato)

Write complete sentences instead.

### Seven Dangerous Expression Formulas

| Pattern | What to watch for |
|---------|-------------------|
| Cinematic Setup | "In a world where [crisis], [virtue] becomes [currency]" |
| Binary Split | "Most people [lazy]. The few who win [disciplined]" |
| Simple Switch | "Stop [old]. Start [new]" |
| Triple Fake Depth | "It's not X. It's not Y. It's Z" |
| FOMO Threat | "If you're not [doing X], you're already [behind]" |
| Invisible Work | "The real [work] isn't [visible]. It's [secret]" |
| Minimalist Smack | "You don't need more [resources]. You need [intangible]" |

### Other Tells
- **Uniform paragraph length.** Vary wildly. One-sentence paragraphs mixed with five-sentence ones.
- **Questions answered immediately.** "What does this mean? It means X." Delete the question.
- **Heavy signposting.** "Now that we've explored X, let's move on to Y." Just move on.
- **Compulsive summaries.** Starting paragraphs with "Overall," or "In conclusion." Cut them.
- **Perfectly symmetrical lists.** Human lists are messier. Vary structure within bullets.
- **Mini-conclusions after every section.** Let the content stand on its own.

## Punctuation and Formatting

- **Em dashes:** Banned. Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- **Excessive bold:** Don't mechanically bold every key term. Use bold sparingly for genuine emphasis.
- **"Header: Description" bullets:** The "**Term:** Definition" format in vertical lists is an AI pattern. Mix up formatting.
- **Emoji in headers:** Never in professional content.
- **Perfect grammar:** Occasional fragments and informal constructions are fine. Suspiciously clean grammar is a tell.

## What Human Writing Sounds Like

### Words and phrases humans actually use
**Discourse markers:** Well, I mean, Look, So, Right, The thing is, I think, sort of, kind of, Fair enough, Granted, That said, Honestly, Frankly, Personally, Actually, Or rather

**Informal intensifiers:** pretty much, basically, way, super, totally, seriously

**Casual connectors:** And, But, So, Plus, Also, Still, Though, Anyway, Besides, Meanwhile, Then again

**Fragments:** Exactly. Obviously. Nope. Not ideal. Big difference. Same old story. For better or worse.

### Characteristics of natural writing
- Register shifts within a single piece (casual to formal and back)
- Contractions everywhere
- Casual asides and occasional tangents
- Real uncertainty: "I don't know," "last I checked," "hard to say"
- First-person perspective with genuine opinion
- Specific references: real events, real people, real numbers
- Emotional texture: humor, frustration, skepticism, excitement
- Repeating the clearest word instead of cycling through synonyms
- Wildly varying paragraph length

## Model-Specific Tells

Watch for these patterns common to assistant-written prose across models:

- **Epistemic hedging:** "I should note that," "it's worth mentioning," "it bears noting"
- **Copula avoidance:** Writing "serves as" or "stands as" instead of just "is"
- **Over-qualification:** Every claim wrapped in caveats and qualifiers
- **Balanced framing:** Presenting "both sides" when a clear stance is more useful
- **Meta-commentary:** Commenting on its own response ("That's a great approach because...")
- **Nested clauses:** Sentences with multiple subordinate clauses stacked together
- **Verbosity without substance:** Using more words without adding more information
- **Explanation over opinion:** Explaining what something is rather than arguing why it matters

## Before/After Examples

**Vague marketing slop:**
> "Unlock the full potential of your marketing strategy with our cutting-edge, AI-powered platform that revolutionizes the way you connect with your audience."

**Human version:**
> "Our platform finds your best-performing ad variants 4x faster than manual testing. Three clients hit 300% ROAS in the first month."

**Generic opener:**
> "In today's ever-evolving digital landscape, businesses need a comprehensive solution that seamlessly integrates into their existing workflows."

**Human version:**
> "You're running campaigns in five tools. This replaces three of them."

**Pompous abstraction:**
> "The vibrant festival stands as a testament to the enduring cultural heritage of the region, showcasing the rich tapestry of traditions."

**Human version:**
> "The festival runs every April since 1987. Last year, 12,000 people attended."

**Throat-clearing:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. The uncomfortable truth is that most teams struggle with the basics."

**Human version:**
> "Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't."

**Rhetorical setup:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? What if the real secret is something entirely different?"

**Human version:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning, not productivity."

**About page slop:**
> "We are passionate about empowering businesses to navigate the complexities of modern marketing and unlock unprecedented growth opportunities."

**Human version:**
> "We help B2B companies get more demos from the traffic they already have. Most clients see results in the first two weeks."

## Self-Check Before Delivering Output

Before presenting any prose, scan for:
1. Em dashes (replace with commas, periods, or parentheses)
2. Tier 1 banned words (replace with plain language)
3. Binary contrast patterns (rewrite as direct statements)
4. Three or more consecutive similar-length sentences (vary them)
5. Filler transitions (delete them)
6. Chatbot artifacts (delete them)
7. Vague claims without specifics (add numbers, names, details)

If you catch yourself writing something that could appear in any document about any topic, rewrite it with specifics only this context would produce.
